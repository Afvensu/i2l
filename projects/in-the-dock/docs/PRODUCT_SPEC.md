# Product Specification — In the Dock

## Purpose
A 15–20 minute professional criminal-defence simulation for MSc Criminology and Security Studies learners. The learner acts as Adaobi Nwankwo, a newly called barrister, and navigates Emeka Chukwuma's case from arrest through verdict.

## Experience goal
Make learners recognise procedural violations at the moment they occur, select appropriate professional remedies, preserve a usable record, and experience how earlier decisions affect later advocacy.

## Learning objectives
1. Evaluate whether an arrest meets the reasonable-suspicion threshold.
2. Diagnose detention, caution, silence and counsel violations.
3. Formulate a statutory rights-based bail submission.
4. Assess a disputed confession under the source's inclusionary-rule framework and formulate the strongest objection.

## Product principles
- Professional reality over quiz mechanics.
- The simulation itself is the assessment environment.
- Maximum five meaningful beats.
- Persistent case state.
- Consequences must be believable and consequential.
- No raw score as the primary learner-facing feedback.
- Preserve the source scenario, terminology and decision logic.

## Audience
MSc Criminology and Security Studies learners.

## Runtime target
15–20 minutes.

## Core mechanic
Professional decision-making under evolving case conditions, with a persistent documentary rights record that changes later situations.
